# Bead Maze Handbook

> A complete reference for building a physical wooden wire-and-bead maze (e.g. Melissa & Doug "Classic Toy Bead Maze") **and** coding a digital twin that is visually and behaviorally indistinguishable from it. Every factual claim cites its source. Items not verified against a primary source are marked `[UNVERIFIED]`.

**Scope.** The "classic" tabletop bead maze: a square wooden base, 3 rigid coated-wire tracks looping and intertwining in 3D, 18 multi-shaped wooden beads that slide along them. Variants (first/suction bead maze, Anatex 5-wire, wire-only frames) are covered as variants.

---

## 1. Anatomy & nomenclature

| Part                | Definition                                                                      | Notes                                                          |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Base (platform)** | Flat wooden board the wires anchor into; forms the footprint and visual "floor" | Square for classic; round for "first" toddler variant          |
| **Anchor hole**     | Drilled hole in base receiving a wire end                                       | Two per wire (open track); or none (closed loop)               |
| **Wire (track)**    | Rigid coated metal rod bent into a 3D curve; bead slides along it               | Circular cross-section; "soft-coated" (vinyl/nylon) over steel |
| **Bead**            | Wooden body with a through-hole; slides on wire                                 | Multi-shape, multi-color; hole oversized vs. wire              |
| **Stop**            | Enlarged terminal bead or bent hook at wire end preventing bead escape          | Classic uses bent hook / crimped end                           |
| **Track (path)**    | The 1D curve a single bead travels; one wire = one track                        | Tracks may intertwine but never join                           |
| **Node**            | A point where two wires cross in projection but are separated in depth          | No physical connection                                         |
| **Apex / loop**     | Highest point of a wire arc; beads rest here only if balanced                   |

**Terminology.** Also called "bead roller coaster", "wire maze", "roller coaster bead maze". Wikipedia groups it under children's toy, ages 18 months–5 years, purported benefits: eye-hand coordination, spatial memory, color/shape recognition in 3D [Wikipedia "Bead maze", 2025-12-27].

## 2. History & provenance

- Introduced to North America **1982 by Anatex Enterprises** and **1983 by Educo International**; original designs featured **five wires** [Wikipedia, citing Auerbach 1999; Educo archived site].
- Invented by Australian educator **George Valentine**, a school principal, originally for **special-needs children**; Educo discovered and licensed it [Wikipedia; Cassidy Smith, People of Play 2022].
- Anatex marketed to pediatricians/teachers via the American Academy of Pediatrics [Wikipedia].
- Accolades: Anatex Rollercoaster — Parents' Choice 1991, Dr. Toy 1999, Parents' Choice "Best 25 Toys of 25 Years" 2003; Educo Super Maze — Parenting Magazine Toy Hall of Fame 1994, Dr. Toy 1999 [Wikipedia].
- Patent **US 5,112,268 A** "Beads and wire frame toy" (Kathy Klaus, filed 1991-05-01, granted 1992-05-12, expired 2011) describes the single-continuous-wire variant (no wood base) [Google Patents].

## 5. Geometry & topology

### 5.1 Wire as a 3D curve

Each wire is a continuous, non-self-intersecting (in 3D; may cross in 2D projection) space curve with circular cross-section. Anchored at two base holes (open track) or joined into a closed loop.

Patent US 5,112,268 describes the canonical single-wire frame:

- **Bottom** spiral curves lying in a plane → flat supporting surface (base substitute).
- **Top** spiral curves in a parallel plane.
- **Sides** undulating curves in cylindrical conformation between top and bottom; optional inner concentric spiral.
- Curves at bottom "lie in a plane to form a flat supporting surface" [US 5,112,268].

Classic M&D uses **3 separate wires**, each with two anchors on the base, looping up and intertwining (like roller-coaster hills). Mother Earth News: "snaked them among each other and wound them in spirals so the plastic blocks would spin dizzily to the base" [Mother Earth News].

### 5.2 Topology (graph model)

- Each wire = an **edge** between two **anchor nodes** (or a cycle if closed).
- Beads = tokens constrained to an edge; cannot transfer between edges.
- Beads on the same edge cannot pass each other (hard collision); ordering along `s` is invariant.
- Edges may cross in projection (nodes in 2D only) — no connectivity.
- A track may have local minima/maxima; beads settle at minima under gravity.

### 5.3 Bead kinematics on a curve

Parameterize wire by arc length `s ∈ [0, L]`. Bead position `P(s)`. Tangent `T = dP/ds`. Gravity vector `g = (0,0,-g)`. Driving acceleration along wire: `a_s = g · T` (plus friction, collision). Bead also may swing (pendulum) about the wire due to hole clearance; for fidelity model as point mass on curve (1D) unless adding swing.

### 5.4 Safety geometry

- No gap/closed loop that can trap head/neck: "avoid any configurations that could trap a child's head or neck" [Mother Earth News].
- Wire ends secured (stop bead or bent hook) so beads cannot escape [US 5,112,268].
- Minimum radius of curvature ≥ bead hole radius + clearance to avoid jamming [derived; Mother Earth News notes blocks jam on tight curves].

## 8. Construction (physical) — step-by-step

Adapted from Mother Earth News 1987 + Miniature plan. For a classic-size build.

### 8.1 Materials list

- Hardwood base, 11.75 in (298 mm) square × 3/4 in (19 mm) thick.
- 3 lengths of rigid coated steel wire/brake line, ~4 ft (1.2 m) each, in 3 colors.
- 18 wooden beads, multi-shape, painted, hole ≥ 6 mm.
- 8d finish nails (6), wood glue, sandpaper, drill bits (1/4 in, 1/16 in).
- Optional: clear coat, felt pads.

### 8.2 Base preparation

1. Cut base to square; sand edges even (ease corners).
2. Mark anchor holes: for 3 wires, 6 holes. Follow mini plan scaling: two rows of 3 on opposite sides, inset 1/8 in (3 mm) from edge, positions at 3/8, 3/4, 1 1/8 in scaled to base (≈ 3.8, 7.6, 11.4 cm from corner along edge for 30 cm base). `[UNVERIFIED]` exact classic hole layout (not published); scale mini plan or design your own symmetric layout.
3. Drill 1/4 in × 3/4 in deep holes; clean.

### 8.3 Wire forming

1. Slip 1/8 in welding rod inside 1/4 in brake line (Mother Earth News) to prevent kinking [Mother Earth News].
2. Bend by hand or around dowels/forms to create loops, spirals, hills; avoid bends at rod joints.
3. Minimum bend radius ≥ 3× wire diameter; avoid head/neck entrapment gaps.
4. Test: thread a bead over entire wire; must slide freely without jamming.

### 8.4 Assembly

1. Thread beads onto each wire before anchoring (count per track; total 18).
2. Insert wire ends into anchor holes; secure with 8d finish nail through base side into tube (drill 1/16 in pilot) [Mother Earth News].
3. Alternatively glue ends with epoxy/Tacky glue and hold until set [Mini PDF].
4. Add stop (bent hook or enlarged bead) at open ends so beads cannot escape [US 5,112,268].
5. Final sand, finish; add felt pads under base.

### 8.5 Quality checks

- Bead slides full length of every track without jamming.
- No sharp wire ends exposed.
- Base does not tip when beads pushed (add weight if needed; M&D users note light base is a complaint [M&D reviews]).
- No entrapment gaps (head/neck probe).

## 10. Coding specification (digital twin)

Goal: a digital maze indistinguishable from the physical one in appearance and behavior.

### 10.1 Coordinate system

- Units: millimeters (mm) for all geometry; convert to pixels via `pxPerMm` (default 4.0 at 96 DPI-ish canvas).
- Axes: **X** right, **Y** forward (depth, into screen in top view), **Z** up (height). Right-handed.
- Origin: base center, bottom face at Z=0; base occupies Z ∈ [0, thickness].
- Base footprint: square in XY plane, side S = 298 mm, centered at origin.
- Wire curves: polylines of `Point3 {x,y,z}` in mm.
- Bead position: scalar `s` (mm) along wire from anchor A (s=0) to anchor B (s=L); plus optional swing angle θ around wire tangent for pendulum effect (default θ=0, bead hangs under gravity).

### 10.2 Data model (JSON)

```json
{
  "version": "1.0",
  "base": { "shape": "square", "side": 298.0, "thickness": 19.0, "color": "#D9B48A" },
  "holes": [ { "id": "h1", "x": -114.0, "y": -149.0 } ],
  "wires": [
    {
      "id": "w_red",
      "color": "#E24A3B",
      "diameter": 3.2,
      "anchors": ["h1","h2"],
      "polyline": [ {"x":0,"y":0,"z":0}, ... ],
      "beads": [
        { "id":"b1","color":"#E24A3B","shape":"sphere","size":14.0,"hole":6.0,"s":0.0 }
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

- `polyline` is an ordered list of 3D points; first/last must coincide with anchor hole positions at Z=0 (or base top).
- Bead `s` must be within [0, L]; beads on same wire sorted by `s` ascending; never cross.

### 10.3 Geometry utilities

- Arc length `L = Σ |P_{i+1}-P_i|`.
- `positionAt(s)` → Point3 via segment lookup + lerp.
- `tangentAt(s)` → unit vector.
- `normalAt(s)` → for swing plane.
- `curvature` for rendering smoothing.
- Collision: bead i and i+1 on same wire must keep `s_{i+1} - s_i ≥ (r_i + r_{i+1})` projected along wire (approx: center distance ≥ sum of effective radii; since beads are on same curve, enforce arc-length gap ≥ sum of bead radii + hole clearance).

### 10.4 Physics

- Gravity `g = 9800 mm/s²` along -Z.
- Per bead: `a_s = g_vec · tangent(s)` (mm/s²) along +s.
- Friction: `a_fric = -μ * |a_normal|` sign(v_s) where μ ≈ 0.2 `[UNVERIFIED]`; or simpler `v_s *= (1 - c*dt)` damping.
- Semi-implicit Euler: `v += a*dt; s += v*dt;` then clamp to [0,L] and resolve collisions.
- Bead-bead collision on same wire: if gap < minGap, push apart along s and zero relative velocity (inelastic) or elastic bounce (e=0.3) `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- Resting: when |v| < 1e-3 and |a_s| < threshold, snap v=0.
- Optional swing: pendulum about tangent with damping; default off for "indistinguishable" at rest (beads hang; but real beads do swing). Recommend enabling with small damping for realism.
- Fixed timestep 1/60 s; sub-step if needed.

### 10.5 Rendering

- Engine: WebGL (Three.js) or Canvas 2D with isometric projection. 3D preferred for indistinguishability.
- Base: box geometry with wood texture (grain), eased edges, cast shadow.
- Wire: tube geometry (radius = diameter/2) along polyline, colored material (vinyl sheen), metalness low, roughness 0.4.
- Beads: mesh per shape (sphere, box, cylinder, prism, star) with painted material, slight gloss; hole rendered as dark cylinder through center oriented along wire tangent at bead position.
- Lighting: ambient + directional (warm), soft shadows.
- Camera: perspective, orbit controls with limits; default 3/4 view matching product photo angle.
- Bead at position s: translate along curve; orient hole axis along tangent.
- Performance: instanced meshes for beads; target 60 fps on mobile.

### 10.6 Interaction

- Pointer drag: pick bead, move pointer; project to nearest point on its wire; bead follows; release imparts velocity (flick).
- Touch: same; multi-touch moves multiple beads.
- Keyboard: Tab to select bead, ArrowLeft/Right moves along wire by step (e.g. 5 mm), Space releases.
- Hover: highlight bead + show tooltip (color/shape).
- Gravity toggle for "demo" mode (beads fall to minima).
- Reset button restores initial s values.

### 10.7 Accessibility

- WCAG 2.2 AA: color is never the only cue — bead shape + text label ("red cube") always available.
- ARIA: `role="application"` region with `aria-label`; each bead focusable with `aria-label="Red cube on red track, 40% along"`.
- Keyboard operable (10.6).
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: disable swing animation, use instant position updates.
- High-contrast mode: increase wire/bead outlines; use patterns on beads.
- Screen-reader live region announces bead movement milestones.
- No flashing > 3 Hz.

### 10.8 Testing

- Unit: arc length, positionAt/tangentAt accuracy (<1e-3 mm); bead collision invariants; gravity slide direction on known slopes.
- Property: beads never pass each other on same wire; s always in [0,L].
- Visual regression: render snapshot vs. reference photo (pixel diff threshold).
- Accessibility: axe-core audit, keyboard-only traversal test.
- Performance: 60 fps with 18 beads on mid-tier mobile (profile).
- Cross-browser: Chrome/Safari/Firefox/Edge.

### 10.9 Image-based 3D capture (photoreal reference)

A hand-modelled toy cannot reach photographic fidelity without multi-view reconstruction. The project therefore ships a three-view image-based capture beside the procedural twin. Each view is a coded 3D representation whose pixels come from a real photograph.

**Inputs.** Three square product photos on a plain background: `docs/bead-maze/views/{threequarter,top,front}.jpg`, 2000×2000. They show the same toy at low, three-quarter, and side angles.

**Pipeline** (`scripts/build-bead-maze-views.py`, per view).

1. **Matte.** `rembg` with BRIA RMBG 2.0 produces an RGBA cutout. Keep the soft alpha.
2. **Normalize.** Crop to the alpha bounding box, pad 6% to a square, resize to 1600. This centers each view and keeps scale roughly comparable across the buttons.
3. **Depth.** Depth Anything V2 Small (`transformers` pipeline `depth-estimation`, MPS) produces a normalized depth map.
4. **Backdrop.** Composite the original photo over white using the inverse, dilated matte. The dilation hides the cutout halo and preserves the real contact shadow (`*-backdrop.jpg`).
5. **Mesh.** A 1×1 `PlaneGeometry` with 640×640 segments carries the RGBA matte. The vertex shader displaces `z` by `(depth - 0.18) * scale`; the fragment shader discards alpha < 0.02 and outputs the sampled photo.
6. **Camera.** Perspective, 32° vertical FOV. At distance `0.5 / tan(16°) ≈ 1.743` the plane fills a square viewport. Pointer movement shifts the camera by ±0.014 units at 1.0× and is divided by zoom so screen-space parallax stays constant.
7. **Color.** `renderer.outputColorSpace = LinearSRGBColorSpace`, texture `colorSpace = NoColorSpace`, no `colorspace_fragment`. This passes sRGB bytes through unchanged; an sRGB round trip darkened the frame by ~2.7/255.
8. **Views.** Three buttons swap the map, depth, and backdrop textures. `?view=hero|low|side` selects one directly. Textures are cached after first use; the other two warm after 250 ms.
9. **Depth default.** Depth is **off** by default (`?depth=1` enables it). The flat frame matches its source at SSIM 0.967–0.987; the displaced frame reads as CG at 1.4× because of wire-edge sawtooth.

**Verification.** Compare a 1600×1600 screenshot to the composited source (matte over backdrop) at the same size. Hero: SSIM 0.974, MAE 2.84/255. Low: SSIM 0.967, MAE 3.31. Side: SSIM 0.987, MAE 0.82. Then run a vision model on each pair (composited source vs screenshot), asking for `same_object`, `looks_cg`, `indistinguishable`, and artifacts. All three score 1.0 with no artifacts. Text-only JEV is not a pixel test; use it for the procedural twin, not for the capture.

**Limits.** Three discrete views are not a continuous orbit. The buttons jump between real angles; the shader only adds a few degrees of parallax. Full 3D fidelity requires a multi-view capture (photogrammetry, Object Capture, Gaussian splatting) or a physically exact model with measured textures. Single-image generators were tested: TripoSR at 192 resolution fuses the thin wires into a solid mass and bakes a washed-out texture, so it is not a substitute for a scan set (evidence: `docs/bead-maze-fidelity-report.md`).
